# Complex Numbers

<section id="introduction" style="padding:20px; background-color:#f0f8ff; border-radius:8px; margin-bottom:15px;">
## Part 1: Introduction to Complex Numbers

Complex numbers are numbers of the form <math>z = a + bi</math>, where:
- <math>a</math> is the **real part**,
- <math>b</math> is the **imaginary part**,
- <math>i</math> is the imaginary unit satisfying <math>i^2 = -1</math>.

They can be represented on a **complex plane**, with the x-axis as the real axis and y-axis as the imaginary axis.
</section>

<section id="operations" style="padding:20px; background-color:#fff0f5; border-radius:8px; margin-bottom:15px;">
## Part 2: Basic Operations

### Addition and Subtraction
If <math>z_1 = a + bi</math> and <math>z_2 = c + di</math>, then:

<mathblock>
z_1 + z_2 = (a+c) + (b+d)i
z_1 - z_2 = (a-c) + (b-d)i
</mathblock>

### Multiplication
<mathblock>
z_1 * z_2 = (a + bi)(c + di) = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i
</mathblock>

### Conjugate
The conjugate of <math>z = a + bi</math> is <math>\overline{z} = a - bi</math>.
</section>

<section id="polar" style="padding:20px; background-color:#f5fff0; border-radius:8px; margin-bottom:15px;">
## Part 3: Polar Form

A complex number can be represented in **polar form** as:

<mathblock>
z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)
</mathblock>

Where:
- <math>r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}</math> is the **magnitude**,
- <math>\theta = \arctan(\frac{b}{a})</math> is the **argument**.

### Euler's Formula
<mathblock>
z = r e^{i\theta}
</mathblock>
</section>

<section id="applications" style="padding:20px; background-color:#fffaf0; border-radius:8px;">
## Part 4: Applications

Complex numbers are used in:
- Electrical engineering (AC circuits)
- Signal processing
- Quantum mechanics
- Fractals and computer graphics

### Example: Multiplying in Polar Form
<mathblock>
z_1 = r_1 e^{i\theta_1}, \quad z_2 = r_2 e^{i\theta_2}
z_1 z_2 = r_1 r_2 e^{i(\theta_1 + \theta_2)}
</mathblock>
</section>
